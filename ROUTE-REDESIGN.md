# Route Redesign — S/V *Sabbatical*: Winthrop Harbor → Old Saybrook

**Boat:** Beneteau Oceanis 30.1 · 4 crew · ~6 kn under power · 130 L / 34 gal diesel · 42 gal water · 14'6" air-draft design target mast-down
**Window:** Summer 2027, 8-week sabbatical (56 calendar days)
**Revision 2** — incorporates three owner decisions: start at **Winthrop Harbor (North Point Marina)** rather than Chicago; **skip Mystic**; and **compress harder, accepting more 70+ nm days**.

---

## A. Rationale

### What revision 2 changed

| Decision | Effect |
|---|---|
| Start at Winthrop Harbor, not Chicago | 25 nm further up the lake at the start, but it forces a 74 nm open crossing on Day 1 — see the warning below |
| Skip Mystic | −2 days; Greenport → Old Saybrook restored as a direct 50 nm finish, still taking Plum Gut and The Race |
| Compress, accepting 70+ nm days | −6 further days |
| **Total** | **52 days → 44 days**, floating pool **4 → 12** |

### The slack decision (unchanged in principle, much stronger in practice)

**44 scheduled days, 12 floating.** Slack now comes in two forms rather than three, because compression consumed the canal accordion:

1. **Five named reserve/layover days as real `ItineraryDay` entries** — Day 6 (Mackinac), Day 12 (Baie Fine second night), Day 14 (Killarney), Day 22 (Cleveland), Day 40 (NYC). Plus two work days at zero distance: Day 26 (mast unstep) and Day 37 (rigging at Catskill).
2. **Twelve unallocated floating pool days**, with a ledger and rules of use in §G.

A pure pool loses to crew psychology — crews read the printed schedule, not a buffer spreadsheet, so a named day makes "we are not moving today" the default instead of something someone has to argue for at 0500. Named days buy *permission*; the pool buys *transferability*. The compression means the pool is now doing most of the work, which is fine: 12 days is enough to absorb two multi-day gales and still finish early.

**Original ask was 10–12 slack days. This delivers 17** (5 named + 12 pool).

### Where the eight days came from

| Cut | Days | What it costs |
|---|---:|---|
| Skip Mystic and its layover | 2 | The *Charles W. Morgan*. Owner's call. |
| Merge St. Joseph and Grand Haven into one Holland stop | 1 | Two southern Michigan harbour towns, now bail-outs. Inherent to the Winthrop Harbor start — St. Joseph is a *southward* detour from there. |
| Drop Pentwater and Frankfort; restore Ludington | 1 | Two small harbours. Ludington is the better fuel/resupply stop anyway, and Big Sable Point still gets rounded on a fresh morning (Ludington sits 8 nm *south* of the point). |
| Drop Gore Bay | 1 | Best provisioning on Manitoulin's north shore — but Little Current, one day later, is better provisioned still. |
| Drop Dunkirk; restore Erie → Tonawanda at 70 nm | 1 | A quiet last US night. Barcelona (20 nm) and Dunkirk (42 nm) survive as named bail-outs. |
| Canal: 10 travel days → 9 | 1 | Pittsford, Lyons, Baldwinsville and Medina as overnights; all become pass-throughs or lunch stops. |
| Hudson: merge Haverstraw back into a single 75 nm Poughkeepsie → NYC day | 1 | Control over the golden-hour arrival. Recoverable — see the Day 39 note. |

### What I did **not** cut, and why

- **The Killarney reserve day (D14).** The Georgian Bay crossing is the highest-probability weather pin on the route. Cutting this is cutting the wrong thing.
- **The Baie Fine second night (D12).** The best day of the voyage.
- **The Cleveland reserve (D22).** Lake Erie remains the most dangerous water on the trip.
- **The Catskill rigging day (D37).** The old plan gave the mast re-step "2–3 hrs." Crane time is 2–3 hours; re-tuning a rig that has lain on deck three weeks, reconnecting masthead instruments and bending on sails is a day — and the Hudson Highlands gust a 12-knot day to 25.
- **The Goderich split on Lake Huron (D17).** This is the one place I resisted compression. Kincardine → Port Huron in one 85 nm hop is an exposed lee shore with two marginal river-mouth bail-outs, ending in a CBP clearance after fifteen hours. It is available as a cut if you want a 43rd day; I recommend against it and it is the last thing on my list.
- **The Waterford Flight staging at Schenectady (D34/D35).** Running Amsterdam → Waterford in one day puts you at Lock E-6 around 1340 against a 2:30 pm cutoff — a 50-minute margin on a 9.5-hour day. That was the original plan's P0 failure and I will not reintroduce it. Staging at Mohawk Harbor puts you at E-6 at 0910.

### Day 1 is now the hardest day of the trip, and it is on day one

Starting at Winthrop Harbor commits you to a long first crossing. The lake is 60+ nm wide there and every Michigan harbour is 70–80 nm away; there is no short first hop that goes the right direction. **Day 1 is 74 nm to Holland, roughly 12–13 hours.** Days 2 and 3 are 74 and 76. That is three consecutive 74–76 nm days at the point when the crew is least worked up and the boat least proven.

Mitigations, all of which should happen:
- **Sail a shakedown out of North Point Marina the afternoon before Day 1** — not an itinerary day, just part of loading the boat. Find your problems 3 nm from the dock, not 35.
- Depart 0430. Sunrise in late June at that latitude is about 0515.
- Chicago (25 nm), Michigan City (50 nm) and St. Joseph (63 nm) are all reachable diversions for the first two-thirds of the crossing.
- This is the widest open water until the Georgian Bay crossing. Treat the forecast as a hard gate, and spend a pool day at Winthrop Harbor rather than leave on a marginal one. You have twelve.

**If you would rather not open with three 75s:** put one pool day back as Pentwater or Frankfort between Days 2 and 3. That converts 74/76 into 62/56/40 and costs one of twelve.

### Distances and departure date

Total **1,671 nm-equivalent** against revision 1's 1,709 and the original plan's 1,711. The drop is the Mystic out-and-back, the Gore Bay and Dunkirk detours, and a more direct Lake Michigan crossing from the new start point.

**Departure must be a Friday.** Day 16 (Tobermory → Kincardine) has to land on a Saturday for the bagpiper, and Day 16 is 15 days after Day 1.

> **Recommended: depart Winthrop Harbor Friday 25 June 2027.** Kincardine Saturday 10 July. Erie Canal 20–29 July, well inside extended lock hours (which ended 10 Sep in 2026). Old Saybrook Saturday 7 August, or 19 August if every pool day is spent.
>
> **Cascading caveat:** Beaver Island's Municipal Marina *North* — the one with the fuel dock — did not open until 29 June in the 2025 season, and Day 4 on this schedule is 28 June. Marina South (25 slips, power and water) opens ~10 June, so you will have a berth but may not get diesel. Fuel at Ludington on Day 2 and again at Leland or Northport. The alternative, departing Friday 2 July, puts Days 1–3 over the 4 July weekend with Holland and Ludington packed — I would take the fuel problem over the crowds.

### Leg union type

**No new `Leg` id required.** All 44 days map onto the existing eight, and each leg occupies one contiguous block of day numbers, which `legGroups()` in `stats.ts` requires:

`lake-michigan` 1–6 · `north-channel` 7–15 · `lake-huron` 16–18 · `st-clair` 19 · `lake-erie` 20–25 · `erie-canal` 26–35 · `hudson` 36–40 · `sound-saybrook` 41–44

### Lake Michigan

**Day 1 — Winthrop Harbor → Holland, 74 nm.**
- highlights: `["The widest open-water crossing of Lake Michigan on the whole route — out of sight of both shores for a couple of hours in the middle", "Lake Macatawa: a two-mile inland lake behind a narrow channel, fully protected in any weather, with 'Big Red' lighthouse on the south pier"]`
- warnings: `["The hardest day of the trip is day one. Sail a shakedown out of North Point Marina the afternoon before — find your problems 3nm from the dock, not 35. Depart 0430", "Diversions for the first two-thirds: Chicago (25nm), Michigan City (50nm), St. Joseph (63nm). Past the midpoint you are committed to the Michigan shore"]`
- notes: `"Starting at Winthrop Harbor puts you 25nm up the lake but commits you to a long first crossing — the lake is 60+nm wide here and there is no short hop that goes the right direction. North Point is the largest marina on the Great Lakes (1,500 slips, gas and diesel at the fuel dock, pump-out) so leave with everything full. Treat the forecast as a hard gate; this is the widest water until Georgian Bay. Both Eldean Shipyard and Yacht Basin Marina on Lake Macatawa take transients and both have diesel and pump-out."`

**Day 2 — Holland → Ludington, 74 nm.**
- highlights: `["The Michigan shore at its most hospitable: Grand Haven, Muskegon, White Lake and Pentwater all passed within reach", "Ludington — the best resupply town on the eastern shore, and the staging harbour for Big Sable Point"]`
- warnings: `["Bail-outs every 12–15nm: Grand Haven (26nm), Muskegon (38nm), White Lake (50nm), Pentwater (63nm). This is why a 74nm day is acceptable here", "Afternoon NW winds build a 2–4ft short-period chop off this shore. Underway by 0500 and aim to be inside the piers by 1600"]`
- notes: `"Long but low-commitment — you are never more than eight miles from a harbour entrance. Ludington sits 8nm SOUTH of Big Sable Point, which is the whole reason it is the overnight: the point gets rounded tomorrow morning on a fresh crew rather than this afternoon on a tired one. Full fuel, water and pump-out here. Last certain diesel before the Manitou Passage."`

**Day 3 — Ludington → Leland, 76 nm.**
- highlights: `["Big Sable Point and Point Betsie rounded in the morning, then Sleeping Bear's 400ft perched dunes to starboard through the Manitou Passage", "Optional: anchor two hours in South Manitou Harbor and dinghy ashore to the Valley of the Giants old-growth cedars and the wreck of the Francisco Morazan (adds ~4nm)"]`
- warnings: `["Bail-outs: Manistee (30nm), Frankfort (48nm), Onekama/Portage Lake (40nm). After Frankfort the shelter thins out until Leland", "Manitou Passage generates confused seas when wind opposes lake swell. If it is up, skip South Manitou and run straight through. South Manitou Harbor is open E and SE and is a fair-weather stop only"]`
- notes: `"The longest day on Lake Michigan and the third 75 in a row — if the crew is flagging, this is the right place to spend a pool day and split it at Frankfort. Big Sable at dawn, Point Betsie mid-morning, then the passage. Leland's Fishtown basin is tiny and does not take reservations reliably; if it is full, anchor in the lee north of the harbour or run 12nm to Northport. Fuel at Leland or Northport — do not count on Beaver Island tomorrow. Carlson's Fish Market for smoked whitefish before they close."`

**Day 4 — Leland → Beaver Island (St. James), 47 nm.**
- highlights: `["Beaver Island: the most isolated inhabited island on the Great Lakes, with a genuine Irish-heritage culture and a nineteenth-century Mormon-kingdom history nobody believes until they read it", "Paradise Bay / St. James Harbor — a deep, protected natural harbour with a light on the point, and courtesy bikes at the marina"]`
- warnings: `["Do not plan on Beaver Island diesel. The fuel dock is at Municipal Marina North, which did not open until 29 June in the 2025 season; Day 4 on a 25 June departure is 28 June. Fill at Leland or Northport", "Rounding Cathead Point off the Leelanau tip: the shoal extends well north of the point and the seas stand up there. Give it a mile, then it is 34nm of open crossing with no shelter"]`
- notes: `"A relief after three 75s, and the first genuinely committing forecast gate of the trip — once past Cathead Point there is nothing until St. James. Ride out to the Beaver Head Light or the Protar cabin on the courtesy bikes. The night sky here is as dark as anything before the North Channel."`

**Day 5 — Beaver Island → Mackinac Island, 42 nm.**
- highlights: `["Grays Reef Passage and the Straits of Mackinac — the funnel where all upper-lakes traffic converges", "Threading under the five-mile Mackinac Bridge with the island ahead: the best landfall on Lake Michigan"]`
- warnings: `["Straits currents run 2–3kt and reverse; a wind-against-current chop in the Straits is short, steep and out of proportion to the wind strength", "Reserve Mackinac Island State Harbor in advance — it is small, it fills, and there is no anchoring refuge if you arrive with it full and a blow coming"]`
- notes: `"Depart St. James at dawn. Grays Reef Passage is well marked but busy with commercial traffic — monitor VHF 16. Because Beaver Island took 35nm out of the old Leland-to-Mackinac run, this arrival happens in the early afternoon with the crew awake and on deck, which is how it should be. Have the coffee up and everybody topside for the bridge."`

**Day 6 — Mackinac Island layover / RESERVE.**
- highlights: `["Fort Mackinac, Arch Rock, and the eight-mile perimeter road by bicycle — no cars on the island", "Last thorough provisioning and fuel before the North Channel"]`
- warnings: `["With Gore Bay cut, Little Current is now SIX days away. Provision to that gap plus three days, not to the next night — Meldrum Bay has basics only and the Benjamins have nothing at all", "Fill diesel, both jerry cans, the water tank and all three water jugs. Confirm every passport and read the CBSA telephone reporting procedure before you need it"]`
- notes: `"Named reserve day and the trip's first hard buffer. If the first five days ran clean, spend it on the island. If they did not — and three 75s will tell — this is where you get level. Top up all fuel and water, buy fresh perishables that will last to Little Current, and eat down what will not. Grand Hotel porch for a drink; jacket after 6pm for men. Verify the Garmin inReach plan covers Canada before you cross."`

### The slack ledger

Keep this as a running page in the log, updated every morning:

```
POOL: 12 days.  Spent: ___  Remaining: ___
```

Rules of use, in order:
1. **Never** spend a pool day to make a weather window. Pool days buy rest, repairs and better light — nothing else.
2. Best places to be stuck: Killarney, Baie Fine, Cleveland, Greenport, Mackinac.
3. **Restoration order** if you want to spend pool days on the trip rather than hold them: (a) split Day 3 at Frankfort if the opening three 75s are too much; (b) put Gore Bay back between Days 8 and 9; (c) stop at Haverstraw on Day 39 for a controlled golden-hour arrival; (d) add a second Erie reserve at Presque Isle; (e) add a Greenport layover for the North Fork.
4. If you are ever tempted to cut the Killarney reserve (D14), the Cleveland reserve (D22), the Goderich split (D17) or the Schenectady Flight staging (D34), don't. Those four are load-bearing.
5. Finishing early is a legitimate outcome. Twelve unspent days is a week at home before work starts again.
